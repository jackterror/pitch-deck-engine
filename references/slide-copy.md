# Edit for the Investor

Run this pass before layout and when reviewing or revising existing copy. The investor should be able to scan the deck, understand the argument, and explain it to a partner.

1. **Name the investor’s question.** Use the stage, audience, and slide’s role in the argument. Ask what this reader needs to believe before continuing. Pick one conclusion that advances the case.
2. **Write the conclusion as the headline.** Say what happened, what changed, or what is being proposed in plain language. Use a concrete actor, action, and outcome when the claim supports them. Replace topic labels and interchangeable adjectives with the most useful specific detail. Keep the wording conversational and the headline within two lines at presentation size.
3. **Choose the proof.** Use the smallest chart, screenshot, comparison, metric, or documented example that makes the point credible. Put evidence where the reader sees it as they read the headline. Keep units, time period, cohort, source, and any material qualification close to the claim. State the evidence level when it affects meaning. For example, a prototype trial establishes use under the trial conditions. Evidence of paid conversion or measured improvement requires a different source.
4. **Cut and place the remaining copy.** Turn founder paragraphs into a headline, proof, and the context needed to interpret it. Put the spoken story, transitions, and delivery cues in speaker notes. Put calculations, methodology, and backup answers in the appendix. Keep information that changes the meaning on the slide itself. Split a slide when it contains two necessary investor conclusions.
5. **Run the reader test.** Read the headlines alone. Each should contribute something distinct, and together they should tell the story. Scan each slide at actual viewing size and paraphrase the point and its proof without the notes. For a send-ahead deck, also check that the slide explains the number or claim on its own. If the point is buried, the evidence fails to support it, or the copy depends on small type or a missing narrator, revise before layout approval.

## Copy limits

Use one sentence for the headline and a visual or a few short lines to support it. As an editing trigger, review slides with more than about 30 visible words for live use or 50 for send-ahead, counting headline and body copy. Chart labels and source lines still need to be readable. These are house targets. Keep necessary context, and make room by cutting repetition or moving a secondary point to another slide.

## Worked example

**Founder draft.** Our company has been doing a lot of work with warehouses to improve truck scheduling. We built a prototype so dispatchers can update arrivals, and three warehouses tried it across 12 deliveries. We haven’t measured whether wait times are down yet, but we’re looking to run paid pilots to figure that out.

**Headline.** Three warehouses tested the prototype.

**Proof on slide.** 12 inbound deliveries in unpaid trials.

**Context for a send-ahead deck.** Dispatchers used a shared schedule to update truck arrivals.

**Notes or appendix.** The team will compare wait times with the existing process in a future pilot. No wait-time reduction has been measured.

The investor can tell what was tested and how much evidence exists. A headline claiming shorter wait times would need the measurement on the slide.
