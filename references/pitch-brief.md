# Pitch Brief and VC Elevator Pitch

Use the brief to decide what the investor should remember. Use the elevator pitch to see whether that case holds up when spoken. Update both if the evidence or round changes.

## Internal pitch brief

Fit the brief on one screen. Pull answers from supplied material before asking the founder. Note material gaps and keep the evidence ledger as the source of truth.

| Decision | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Audience and action | Stage, investor type or named audience, what the investor already knows, and the desired next step. |
| Company sentence | Name the customer, the problem or job, and what the product does about it. Use everyday language and one sentence where practical. |
| Investment thesis | Why this could become a meaningful business and why the team has a credible path to build it. |
| Hook and proof | One memorable reason to pay attention, its strongest supporting claim ID, and any qualification that must survive compression. |
| Business and stage | Product status, who pays, and how. Separate validated economics from planned pricing. |
| Round and milestone | Confirmed amount and currency, what the money buys, what becomes measurable, and by when if supported. |
| Hard question | The biggest remaining reason to pass and the evidence that would answer it. |

Choose the hook by comparing the strongest plausible angles. Start with the specific customer problem and your insight by default. Favor traction when observed results are material and interpretable. Favor founder insight or a technical breakthrough when a concrete experience or result supports it. Favor market timing when a dated change makes the product possible or urgent. An analogy can speed comprehension if the mapping is accurate. The choice shapes the emphasis and spoken opener. The deck still follows the default order unless a change earns its place.

## Spoken VC pitch

Write a conversational version that can be said in roughly 45 to 60 seconds. Introduce the speaker when the listener has not met them. Use the following beats and combine them when natural.

1. Say what the company does and who it serves.
2. Describe the customer's pain and what changes with the product.
3. Explain why this approach can win. Use a concrete mechanism, insight, or example.
4. State the business model and current stage. Give one interpretable proof point.
5. State the raise, its use, and the measurable milestone if confirmed.
6. End with a next step appropriate to the conversation, such as a product walkthrough.

If the user wants a 30-second version, keep the company sentence, hook, one proof point, and next step. Preserve important qualifications. When a metric or raise is missing, record it in the private brief, and use the actual stage or a meeting request in outward copy. Keep assumed pricing and planned milestones explicit.

## Rehearse the hook

When the opening is weak, try one or two sentences led by the problem, the product, or the people behind it. Pick the version that makes the case clearest and gives a listener a reason to ask a follow-up question. Keep stronger proof in the opening when it earns the attention.

Deliver the pitch aloud to someone outside the venture and check the timing. Ask them to retell the company, the strongest proof, and the next step in their own words. Ask what held their interest and what they still need explained. Revise where the story or a qualification gets lost. Without a listener, do a cold read and leave human rehearsal as a follow-up.

## Optional investor one-pager

When requested, turn the same claims into a standalone page a reader can forward. Include the company sentence, customer problem and solution, one or two evidence points, business and market context, the relevant team advantage, and the round or next step. Include short source or time labels where needed. Keep private objections, placeholder tags, and internal notes in the working brief.
