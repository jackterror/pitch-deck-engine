# Investment Case

## Default Slidebean sequence

Follow the order of the video and use the written guide for optional details. Combine slides that share evidence. Add or move a slide when an investor needs the information at that point in the argument. Aim for roughly 10 to 15 core slides for a first read.

| Slide | Investor question and supporting evidence |
|---|---|
| 1. Cover | What do you do, and for whom? Name the user or buyer and outcome in a concrete sentence. |
| 2. Problem | Who feels the pain, and what do they do today? Show a current workflow, buyer behavior, or measurable cost. |
| 3. Solution | How do you change that outcome? Show how the approach answers the problem and what insight makes it work. Add a timing argument here or beside it when a dated market shift matters. |
| 4. Product | Can I see how it works? Show one representative workflow, screenshot, or brief demo. When customer value is central to the case, compare the same job before and after in a unit the buyer cares about. Label prototypes, mockups, and concept visuals. |
| 5. Business model | Who pays, for what, and how much? Explain the pricing unit and the economics of a representative sale or transaction where known. |
| 6. Traction | What have you proven? Show the most relevant observed adoption, paid conversion, retention, customer outcomes, or technical progress. If traction is limited, use accurately labeled validation or learning and state what is still unproven. |
| 7. Go-to-market | How will you reach your first customers and scale? Name the initial segment, channel, sales motion, and next test. Separate a proven channel from a hypothesis. |
| 8. Market size | How large can this become? Use qualifying buyers or transactions × supported spend or fee. Show the initial reachable segment and a credible expansion path. |
| 9. Competition | What do customers choose today? Include doing nothing, internal builds, and incumbent workflows where material. Compare using buyer criteria. |
| 10. Competitive advantage | Why will customers choose you, and what gets harder to copy? Support the current edge and distinguish it from a future moat. |
| 11. Team | Why can this team win? Show relevant work shipped, domain access, or firsthand insight. Separate prior accomplishments from this venture's traction. |
| 12. Financials | How does the business develop from here? Show the key operating drivers and the financial path they imply. Put assumptions and scenarios in the appendix. If a forecast lacks usable inputs, surface the gap and use a supported operating plan where possible. |
| 13. Ask | What will this round unlock? Show amount and currency, use of proceeds tied to measurable milestones, timing or runway where supported, and the desired next step. |

Use the written guide’s standalone validation, target audience, or roadmap slides when the evidence warrants them. Keep relevant timing, insight, and milestone information visible even when it fits on another slide. Move a strong traction slide forward only when it makes the case clearer and more persuasive.

## Calibrate to the stage

- **Pre-seed.** Show the founder insight, prototype, experiments, or customer discovery and what funding will prove. Give the strength of each signal. Interviews, interest, waitlists, letters of intent, unpaid pilots, and paid deployments carry different weight.
- **Seed.** Show the strongest observed adoption, usage, retention or paid demand available. Explain the first credible acquisition motion, early economics where measured, and what must become repeatable.
- **Series A.** Show how the engine performs over time. Prefer relevant cohorts, customer concentration where material, repeatable acquisition, economics, and an operating plan tied to the evidence. Put the strongest metric up front and show its drivers later.

## Keep numbers defensible

- Assign each claim an evidence state such as actual, third-party fact, derived estimate, forecast, or unverified. Record source, date, and calculation separately. Founder-supplied data may be useful with provenance even when independent confirmation is unavailable.
- Preserve units, cohort, denominator, time range, and baseline. Distinguish ARR from bookings and run rate, and GMV from recognized revenue. Keep pipeline stages distinct from closed revenue. Match time frames across comparisons.
- For a before-and-after customer outcome, identify the current alternative and compare equivalent workloads under comparable conditions. Give the sample or measurement period where relevant. Label modeled improvements as estimates and record the baseline measurement needed if it is missing.
- Show market ranges or sensitivities when key assumptions drive the answer. Avoid treating industry spend, costs of the problem, or an arbitrary market share as addressable revenue.
- Tie projections to drivers, capacity, spend, and timing. Reconcile the deck, model, and ask. Show actuals separately from projections. If runway is stated, reconcile cash, expected burn, planned changes, and the round. Flag unresolved discrepancies before release.

## Add a section when it changes the investment decision

- **AI.** When model performance or cost drives the thesis, show the workflow outcome and benchmark method. Explain repeat usage, cost to serve, human review, and model or data dependencies where they materially affect economics or defensibility.
- **Gaming.** Show the playable loop and its status. Use build-specific playtest or cohort evidence, with acquisition and monetization assumptions appropriate to the stage. Put feature catalogs, character rosters, and detailed game economies in backup.
- **Token-based product.** Explain why a token is necessary for product behavior and separate token activity, incentives, and company revenue. When material to the raise, state what is being raised and the milestone it funds. Keep supply, allocations, vesting, and emissions in backup unless needed to evaluate the deal. Treat unverified terms, legal status, and returns as open issues and route material claims for qualified review.
- **Deep tech or regulated product.** Separate demonstrated performance from unproven technical or approval milestones. Show what evidence, funding, and time retire the next risk. For marketplaces, hardware, or other models with distinct bottlenecks, choose the metrics that reveal those bottlenecks.

## Stress-test before layout

Write the case in one paragraph an investor could forward to a partner. Then list the three most material questions that paragraph leaves open. For each question, provide a sourced answer, a clearly labeled assumption, or the evidence needed to answer it. Fix gaps affecting the decision or label the deck as a draft.

- **Test the market conditions.** Identify the outside condition the case depends on most, such as regulation, distribution access, input costs, or buyer budgets. Ask how the team would respond if it moved against the company. Record the assumption and response in the working brief or diligence notes. Bring it into the deck when the investor needs it to assess the case.
- **Work backward from the funding milestone.** Identify the uncertainty this round needs to resolve and the result that would resolve it. Specify how the team would measure it, what work it takes, and what that work costs using the operating plan. If a smaller experiment can answer the same question, surface it in the brief. If the amount is set but the math or decision threshold is missing, flag the gap for the founder. Keep the ask, test, and measurable result consistent across the pitch and deck.
