# Minimal Slide Design

Apply after the story and evidence pass. Use an approved company brand or reference template when supplied. Otherwise use a quiet 16:9 baseline with white backgrounds, near-black type, light gray chart panels, and one restrained accent. Use a portable sans-serif stack and fonts the recipient can open. Treat color as emphasis or data encoding.

## Use a layout that fits the proof

- **Opening or close.** Large statement, a single image or a small group of related figures, and a clearly visible next step on the close.
- **Product.** One cropped workflow or demo still with a short callout. Label what is live. Provide a still or text fallback for embedded or linked video.
- **Key metric.** Large figure with unit, period, and context. Use a chart when the trend or comparison is the story. Label series directly when practical, and preserve scale, denominator, period, and source.
- **Market, milestones, or competition.** Use a short calculation, timeline, or comparison with legible labels. Use a quadrant only when its axes reflect actual buyer priorities and positions can be defended.

Keep a consistent grid and generous outer margins. Start around 36–48 pt for main headlines, 20–28 pt for supporting type, and 12–14 pt for compact source lines in standard widescreen slides. Check the real export at its intended reading size and split dense content before shrinking it. Use high contrast for footnotes and chart labels, and include labels or shapes when color encodes meaning.

## Match the delivery

A live pitch can rely on the speaker for transitions and examples. A send-ahead deck needs enough on-slide context to stand alone, including what a number measures and what happens next. Keep extra methodology in the appendix with a readable short source line next to the claim. Use selectable text and editable charts where practical.

## Verify the export

Inspect every rendered slide in sequence and at presentation size. Check clipping, contrast, hierarchy, image and chart legibility, source labels, links, demo fallback, appendix references, and numeric consistency with the approved slide plan. Fix the export and inspect it again. Check any distributed version for stray placeholders, private notes, and unapproved customer names or assets.

For the default paired delivery, export an editable `.pptx` and a shareable `.pdf` from the same finished deck. Preserve editable text and native charts and tables where supported. Export the PDF as slides without speaker notes. Verify slide count and order, legibility, figures, and links in both files. After revisions, regenerate both from the same version and label variants clearly if the user requested separate live and send-ahead decks. If a tool cannot produce one format, use an available conversion route or report which file is pending and provide any supported export path.
